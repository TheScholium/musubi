A Zettelkasten ("slip box") is a set of small notes joined by links. The sociologist Niklas Luhmann kept one with about 90,000 slips and called it his partner in thought.

## Five working rules

1. **One idea per note.** A note you can link to precisely is a note you can reuse. Split a note when its title stops describing all of it.
2. **Your own words.** Writing the idea again is the test that you understand it. Quotations belong in literature notes, such as [@gettier1963].
3. **Link on purpose.** Each new note should connect to at least one old note. Say *why* with a typed link: supports::[[Justified true belief analysis of knowledge]] is more useful than a bare link.
4. **Stable addresses.** Each note has a permanent ID (see the note header). Titles can change; links survive.
5. **Structure notes.** A note such as [[Map: Epistemology]] orders other notes into a line of thought. It is an entry point, not a folder.

## Why it works for theory-heavy study

Mathematics, physics and philosophy are *dependency* subjects: a theorem needs definitions, a derivation needs a principle, an objection needs a position. Typed links such as `depends-on` and `objects-to` record those dependencies, and the graph view shows them.

Q: What is the test that a note is atomic?
A: Its title states the whole content, and another note can link to it without linking to unrelated material.
