replies-to::[[Gettier problem]]

:::reply[No false lemmas]
Add a fourth condition: $S$'s belief is not inferred from any false belief. In Case I, Smith infers his belief from the false belief that Jones will get the job, so the case is excluded.
:::

This reply is usually credited to Michael Clark (1963).

:::objection[Fake-barn county]
Henry drives through a district full of barn façades and, by luck, looks at the one real barn. He believes "that is a barn" directly from perception, with no inference and so no false lemma. Many judge that he does not know it. So the fourth condition is not sufficient either.
:::

The case is from Goldman (1976), who credits Carl Ginet.

## Where this leaves the debate

The problem moves from "which falsehood?" to "what kind of luck?". That leads to safety and sensitivity conditions, and to virtue epistemology.
