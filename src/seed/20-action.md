:::principle[Stationary action]
Among all paths $q(t)$ with fixed end points $q(t_1)$ and $q(t_2)$, the physical path makes the action
$$
S[q] = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} L(q, \dot q, t)\, \dd t
$$
stationary: $\delta S = 0$ for every variation that vanishes at the end points.
:::

For a particle in a potential, $L = T - V$.

## Domain of validity

All of classical mechanics with forces from a potential, and (with suitable Lagrangians) field theory and general relativity. The path is *stationary*, not always a minimum.

## Consequences

- The local form of the principle is the [[Euler–Lagrange equations]].
- Symmetries of $S$ give conservation laws: [[Noether's theorem]].
