refutes::[[Justified true belief analysis of knowledge]]

Gettier [@gettier1963] gives two cases where a person has a justified true belief that is not knowledge.

**Case I.** Smith has strong evidence that Jones will get the job and that Jones has ten coins in his pocket. He infers: *the man who will get the job has ten coins in his pocket.* In fact Smith gets the job, and Smith, without knowing it, has ten coins in his own pocket.

:::argument[Gettier against JTB]
P1. If the JTB analysis is correct, every justified true belief is knowledge.
P2. Smith's belief in Case I is true, believed and justified. [Case I]
P3. Smith's belief in Case I is not knowledge. [intuition]
C. The JTB analysis is not correct. [from P1–P3]
:::

## The two principles the cases use

1. One can be justified in believing a false proposition.
2. If $S$ is justified in believing $P$, deduces $Q$ from $P$, and accepts $Q$ on that basis, then $S$ is justified in believing $Q$.

## Replies

- [[No-false-lemmas reply]]

Q: In Gettier's Case I, what makes Smith's belief true?
A: Smith himself gets the job and has ten coins in his pocket, not Jones.
