## Thesis

$S$ knows that $p$ if and only if

1. $p$ is true,
2. $S$ believes that $p$, and
3. $S$ is justified in believing that $p$.

A version appears in Plato's *Theaetetus* (201c–d): knowledge is true belief together with an account (*logos*).

## Main arguments for

- Each condition seems necessary: we cannot know falsehoods, know what we do not believe, or know by a lucky guess.

## Main arguments against

- objects-to::[[Gettier problem]] shows the conditions are not sufficient.

## Proponents

The analysis was often treated as standard before 1963, although historians dispute how many philosophers held it in this exact form.
